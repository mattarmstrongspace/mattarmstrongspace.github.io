First Impressions.

Arrival, adjustment and the beginning of the Global Leadership Study Tour.

BY MATT ARMSTRONG NOVEMBER 2026 EARLY IMPRESSIONS

IN THIS ENTRY

A Different Everyday Rhythm. Learning Beyond The Classroom. Understanding Beyond Words. Adapting My Perspective.

REFLECTION FRAMEWORK

01 / DESCRIBE 02 / ANALYSE 03 / INTEGRATE
← Back to all entries

Returning to Japan for the study tour has been a different experience from my previous visits. Having already spent considerable time in the country, including the past two months exploring different parts of Japan, I arrived with some familiarity with its culture and everyday life. However, this time I am experiencing Japan from a different perspective. Rather than travelling through the country, I am here as a student, with the opportunity to learn, interact and immerse myself more deeply in Japanese culture.

01 / ARRIVAL & ADJUSTMENT

Returning with a different perspective.

My first impressions of Japan have been shaped by previous experiences, so arriving this time did not carry quite the same sense of unfamiliarity as my first visit. Over the past two months, I have had the opportunity to explore Japan more independently, particularly spending time travelling through rural areas and experiencing parts of the country that are quite different from its major cities.

I have found that I particularly enjoy rural Japan. There is something about the slower pace of everyday life and the way people interact that I find appealing. People tend to be a little more gentle in their interactions, and I appreciate the less hurried atmosphere. Comparing this with the energy of a city like Tokyo has made me more aware of how different everyday experiences can be within the same country.

What makes this visit different is the opportunity to experience Japan beyond tourism. Being here as a student gives me a reason to engage more closely with the culture, understand different perspectives and become more involved in everyday life. I am interested in seeing how this experience changes my understanding of a country I have already visited several times.

02 / THE STUDY TOUR EXPERIENCE

A welcoming start to something new.

One of the things that stood out to me on the first day was the enthusiasm of both my cohort and the professors at Tokyo City University. There was a genuine sense of excitement surrounding the start of the study tour, which made the experience feel welcoming from the outset.

What particularly stood out was how willing the professors were to spend time talking with us, even outside the formal learning environment. During our luncheon, they made an effort to interact with us and share their enthusiasm about the university and what was available throughout the programme. Although these were relatively simple interactions, they helped create an environment where I felt genuinely welcomed and that our presence was appreciated.

This initial experience has helped establish a positive impression of the university and the programme. It reminded me that learning in an international environment is not just about formal activities or information being presented. Informal conversations and the willingness of people to engage with us can be just as valuable in helping us feel comfortable and encouraging us to participate.

I am excited about the opportunities ahead and looking forward to making the most of the experience, particularly through the interactions, activities and opportunities to learn from the people around me.

03 / LANGUAGE & CULTURAL AWARENESS

Understanding beyond words.

Having visited Japan several times and spent an extended period here, I have become more comfortable with being in an environment where Japanese is the primary language. However, experiencing Japan as a student rather than a tourist changes the importance of communication. There is a greater opportunity to engage with people, participate in activities and learn through interactions rather than simply navigating everyday situations.

One thing I want to continue developing throughout the tour is my ability to recognise and adapt to differences in communication. Understanding another culture involves more than learning a language or knowing basic social etiquette. It also means being willing to observe, listen and consider the context in which people communicate. I think this is particularly important when interacting with people whose experiences and expectations may differ from my own.

I want to approach these interactions with an open mind, recognising that communication does not always work in the same way I am accustomed to. Being willing to listen, observe and adapt is something I consider important when learning to interact with people from different cultural backgrounds.

04 / GLOBAL LEADERSHIP REFLECTION

The value of an open perspective.

One of the things I value about travelling and experiencing different cultures is the opportunity to challenge my own assumptions. Even though Japan is not an entirely unfamiliar place to me, experiencing it through an academic programme requires me to approach it from a different perspective. I am no longer simply deciding where to go or what to experience as a traveller, but participating in an environment where I have the opportunity to learn from others.

The welcome we received from the professors also made me consider the importance of inclusiveness when working with people from different backgrounds. Their enthusiasm and willingness to spend time with us helped establish a comfortable environment where we felt encouraged to engage. In a leadership context, I think this highlights how relatively small interpersonal actions can influence whether people feel included and comfortable contributing.

This connects with the idea of cultural intelligence, particularly the ability to adapt when working across different cultural environments. Developing this understanding requires curiosity and a willingness to recognise that my own experiences are not necessarily representative of everyone else's. It is something that will become increasingly important as I progress through my studies and eventually work in software engineering, where collaboration with people from different backgrounds is common.

I am looking forward to what the study tour has to offer and the opportunity to develop a deeper understanding of Japanese culture, both inside and outside the university. Having already experienced Japan as a traveller, I am particularly interested in discovering what I can learn from experiencing it as a student.
