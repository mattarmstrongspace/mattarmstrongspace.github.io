Wearing History.

Exploring the history, symbolism and continuing cultural significance of the kimono.

BY MATT ARMSTRONG NOVEMBER 2026 CULTURAL & HISTORICAL UNDERSTANDING

IN THIS ENTRY

Taking Part in a Tradition. Understanding Its History. The Experience of Wearing It. Cultural Appreciation.

One of the more memorable cultural experiences of the study tour was an afternoon spent learning about and wearing traditional Japanese kimono. We were welcomed by a volunteer organisation that helped us get dressed before taking photographs with the rest of the cohort. The afternoon also included a lecture about the history and construction of kimono, which gave me a greater appreciation for a tradition I had previously known relatively little about.

01 / THE KIMONO EXPERIENCE

Taking part in a tradition.

The afternoon began with volunteers helping everyone get dressed in traditional kimono. It was interesting seeing the different styles and patterns, and watching the whole cohort gradually get ready. Once everyone was dressed, we had photographs taken together, which was a great way to capture the experience as a group.

What made the experience particularly enjoyable was having the opportunity to spend the afternoon actually wearing the clothing, rather than simply observing it. Although it was something quite unfamiliar to me, the volunteers and lecturers made the experience welcoming and encouraged us to embrace it.

02 / HISTORY & CULTURAL SIGNIFICANCE

Understanding what it represents.

After getting dressed, we attended a lecture about the history of kimono in Japan. One of the interesting points discussed was its historical connection to Chinese clothing traditions and how the style developed over time into something distinctly associated with Japanese culture.

We also learned about how a traditional kimono is constructed. One detail that stood out to me was that it is made from eight separate pieces of fabric. I had not really considered how much thought and work went into producing a garment like this. Learning about its construction helped me appreciate that a kimono is much more than just a piece of traditional clothing.

03 / TRADITION IN CONTEMPORARY JAPAN

A living cultural practice.

An aspect of the afternoon that I found particularly interesting was the lecturers encouraging us to feel Japanese while wearing the kimono. Initially, I found this difficult. It was unfamiliar to put myself into that frame of mind, particularly when I was conscious that I was a visitor experiencing a tradition from another culture.

However, I did my best to embrace what they were encouraging us to do. As the afternoon went on, I became much more comfortable wearing the kimono and found myself genuinely enjoying the experience. I could understand why people would want to wear kimono from time to time, whether for a special occasion or simply to enjoy wearing something so closely connected to Japanese tradition.

04 / CULTURAL APPRECIATION & LEADERSHIP

Respect through understanding.

Looking back, the experience showed me how participating in a cultural tradition can provide a different kind of understanding compared with simply learning about it. The lecture gave us the historical background, but actually wearing the kimono made the experience more personal and memorable.

I also took away the importance of being open to experiences that initially feel unfamiliar. It was not necessarily easy to put myself into the mindset the lecturers encouraged, but making the effort allowed me to become more comfortable and appreciate the afternoon for what it was.

From a global leadership perspective, I think this connects to the importance of cultural awareness and being willing to engage with different perspectives. Understanding another culture involves more than recognising its customs; it also means being respectful, listening and making an effort to participate when the opportunity is offered.

Overall, it was an afternoon I thoroughly enjoyed and one of the experiences that helped make the study tour more meaningful.
